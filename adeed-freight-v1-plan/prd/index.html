1. Summary

ADEED Freight is a new portal inside the ADEED platform that lets government and enterprise shippers move road freight through a governed marketplace, with TruKKer's platform running execution behind an ADEED-owned experience. Shippers post demand once and get a transparent price, a verified carrier, a digital waybill under every movement, live status, proof of delivery, and an invoice they can accept in the same place. Carriers and drivers enter a National Registry that gives them a portable, verified identity. ADEED operates the control tower and reports the pilot to the Group CEO office.

The product is built on one integration seam. Every call to TruKKer passes through the ADEED Integration Gateway; no user ever touches a TruKKer surface. ADEED holds the governance data (accounts, registry, procurement audit, waybills, documents); TruKKer runs the freight (sourcing, assignment, tracking, invoicing).

Three release horizons govern this document:

  • Pilot, 17 September 2026. A thin, honest slice for the anchor clients: shipper demand and tracking on the live TruKKer engine, carrier onboarding into the registry, an ops console, and a programme dashboard. Several steps are ops-mediated where TruKKer's API does not yet expose them.
  • Enhancement sprints, 21 September to 13 November. Four two-week sprints that turn the slice into the full portal experience and absorb TruKKer's remaining contracts as they land.
  • Phase 1 federal MVP, target January 2027, and Phase 2 mandate readiness beyond it: registry at scale, the e-Bayan issuance engine, regulator views, smart-gate integration.

The experience target is deliberate: a portal that feels like a calm, precise instrument rather than a logistics back office. One accent colour, a white ground, typography doing the hierarchy, and a small set of signature objects (the shipment strip, the bid ledger, the waybill card) reused everywhere so the product reads as one system. Bilingual English and Arabic with full right-to-left from the first screen.

2. Goals, non-goals and success measures

2.1 Goals

  1. Give shippers a single governed place to post demand, see a transparent price, confirm, track, receive POD and accept invoices.
  2. Put a verified carrier, driver and truck behind every movement through the National Registry, mastered in ADEED.
  3. Issue an ADEED waybill (e-Bayan v0) under every pilot movement.
  4. Keep the full procurement audit trail (every quote, every bid, every confirmation) in ADEED.
  5. Operate the network from an ADEED control tower with the pilot KPIs live for the programme office.
  6. Prove the integration model: one gateway, polling first, copy on receipt, one shared identifier.

2.2 Non-goals

  • Replacing TruKKer's execution stack. Sourcing, assignment, telematics, invoicing and collections stay in TruKKer.
  • Carrier bidding inside the ADEED carrier portal. TruKKer declined the bid API (GAP-21); carriers keep bidding on TruKKer's loadboard.
  • A driver-facing ADEED app in the pilot. Drivers stay in TruKKer's app until Phase 1.
  • Regulator and inspector surfaces in the pilot. They are Phase 1 onward.
  • Payment execution inside the portal. Payments run on the 7X financial rails; the portal records and reflects them.
  • Any TruKKer-branded surface inside the ADEED experience.

3.3 Access model

All human users authenticate through the existing ADEED session (magic link or password). Freight access is granted by a FreightParticipant row with a freight role (shipper_planner, shipper_procurement, shipper_finance, carrier, ops, programme, later regulator), mirroring the Finance portal's FinanceParticipant precedent so one email can hold several ADEED roles. Machine access (client ERPs) uses the Partner API key model with new freight scopes.

4. Scope and releases

4.1 Pilot, 17 September 2026

The pilot is a working slice on the live TruKKer engine for two to three public-sector anchor clients and their current carriers. It is honest about what TruKKer's API exposes today.

Must have:

  • Shipper accounts, KYB, verification, joint provisioning to TruKKer (v2 client contracts), team roles.
  • Demand capture through the RFQ wizard with templates; completeness checks; submission creates the TruKKer inquiry; amend and cancel before confirmation (v2).
  • Price and bids visible to the shipper: transparent bid board mirrored from TruKKer, the auto-declared winner, price, quote reference. Client confirmation recorded by ops in TruKKer and mirrored by ADEED, until the self-serve write exists.
  • Shipments mirrored with ADEED references; assignment (truck and driver) visible.
  • Waybill v0: an ADEED waybill ID issued per movement with a QR-verifiable record, attached to the order as soon as TruKKer confirms the hook (GAP-19).
  • Status timeline from the Annex A dictionary by polling shipment details; notifications per milestone by email.
  • POD pulled and copied on receipt into UAE-resident storage; document vault; line items.
  • Invoice per order with PDF (v2), statement of account and unpaid invoices; acceptance and disputes are read-only until the write lands.
  • Carrier onboarding wizard writing to the v2 supplier, driver and truck contracts; ops verification queue; registry credential and quality score v0.
  • Ops console: pipeline, exceptions, SLA board, registry queue, integration health.
  • Programme dashboard: the pilot measures on one page, exportable.
  • Bilingual EN/AR, RTL, WCAG AA.

Ops-mediated in the pilot (with a portal state that reflects the outcome): client price confirmation, incident logging, waybill attachment if GAP-19 slips.

5. Experience principles and design direction

5.1 Principles

  1. One system, one language. Every surface (shipper, carrier, ops, programme) shares the same shell, type, spacing and objects. Nothing looks bolted on.
  2. State is the interface. The product is mostly about where things are: a request, a shipment, a document, an invoice. State is shown in form (a strip, a chip, a stripe), never only as a word.
  3. Quiet by default, loud for exceptions. Colour appears only where something needs attention or can be clicked. The blue is for actions and ADEED-owned identity; red is reserved for breaches; nothing decorative.
  4. Show the evidence. Every price carries its bids, every status its timestamp, every document its receipt time. Trust is built by showing, not stating.
  5. Arabic is a first language, not a translation. Layout is designed in both directions; Arabic copy is written, not translated word for word.
  6. Nothing fake. No placeholder numbers, no invented savings, no cosmetic motion. If data is missing, the interface says so and says why.

5.2 Visual direction

The direction is a precise, calm instrument: white ground, ink typography, a single brand accent, and data-first surfaces with generous rhythm. It should feel closer to a well-made financial terminal redesigned by a product studio than to a logistics dashboard.

5.3 Signature objects

These are the reusable objects the design phase must define first, because every screen composes from them.

  1. Shipment strip. A horizontal state strip that renders the Annex A sequence (Upcoming to Completed; cross-border adds the border states) as segments with the current segment filled, timestamps on hover, planned versus actual underneath. It is the same object in list rows, the shipment hero, the ops board and notifications.
  2. Bid ledger. The transparent bid board as a ledger: anonymised bidders until award, price, trucks, validity, time received, and the auto-declared winner marked with the quote reference. It is an audit record first and a table second.
  3. Waybill card. The ADEED waybill as a physical-feeling card: EBN reference in mono, QR, shipper, consignee, cargo, route, carrier, driver, truck, validity window, verification state. Printable.
  4. Registry credential. The carrier or driver identity card: registry ID, verification state, quality score, expiries, AEO track.
  5. Vault tile. A document as a tile with type, source (TruKKer or ADEED), received time and the copy-on-receipt marker.
  6. Control board. The ops network view: pipeline counts, SLA countdowns (6-hour POD, 1-hour incident, 72-hour onboarding), the exception queue, integration health, all readable in ten seconds.
  7. Onboarding wizard. One wizard pattern for shipper and carrier onboarding: numbered steps on the left, one question group per screen, progress saved, documents with expiry extraction.

5.4 Layout system

  • Portal shell: a 240 px collapsible rail, a page header with the title, a one-line context and at most one primary action, content at a maximum width of 1280 px.
  • Lists are rows with hairline separators, not card grids; detail views open in place or as a drawer, never a modal for reading.
  • Density is operational: 15 px body, 13 px secondary, tabular numerals, 44 px minimum row height.
  • Mobile: the rail becomes a top bar and drawer; every list and strip collapses to one column; carrier onboarding is designed mobile-first.

5.5 Bilingual and right-to-left

  • Logical CSS properties throughout (padding-inline, margin-inline-start, inset-inline), no left or right in component styles.
  • Direction is set at the locale layout; the shipment strip, wizard and ledger are mirrored, not flipped.
  • Arabic copy is authored by a native writer from the same content model; numerals stay Western Arabic in both languages for consistency with references and invoices.
  • Every screen is reviewed in Arabic before it is signed off.

5.6 Accessibility and content

  • WCAG 2.2 AA: contrast, focus visible, keyboard paths through the wizard, strip and ledger, reduced-motion and reduced-transparency respected.
  • Copy: plain sentences, active voice, no jargon a shipper would not use. Controls say what happens ("Confirm price", then "Price confirmed"). Errors say what went wrong and what to do.
  • Empty, loading and error states are designed for every list and object: an empty vault says what will appear and when; a shipment with no status yet says "awaiting the first update from TruKKer".

5.7 What to avoid

Purple or gradient accents; dark-mode sections inside a light product; three-equal-card feature rows; decorative dots and status colours on every row; eyebrow labels above every heading; fake precision in numbers; em dashes in copy; any surface that reads as a marketing page.

7. Functional requirements

Each requirement lists what the user can do, what the system does, the acceptance criteria, and the TruKKer endpoints involved (API v2 names). "Ops-mediated" marks pilot steps where an ADEED ops user records an outcome that TruKKer's API does not yet expose.

FR-1 Access and accounts

  • FR-1.1 Users sign in with the existing ADEED magic link or password; freight access requires a FreightParticipant row. Users without one see a request-access page.
  • FR-1.2 Role-gated navigation and API guards for every freight role; an 8-hour inactivity timeout on freight sessions, as the Finance portal does.
  • FR-1.3 Shipper admins invite planners, procurement and finance users; invitations create the participant row and send a magic link.
  • Acceptance: a user with two ADEED roles switches portals without re-authenticating; a revoked participant loses access within one request.

FR-2 Shipper onboarding and joint provisioning

  • FR-2.1 Invitation or sign-up creates a shipper organisation pending verification.
  • FR-2.2 KYB captures trade licence, TRN, billing entities and signatories with document upload and expiry extraction.
  • FR-2.3 Ops verifies; on approval the gateway provisions the TruKKer client: Shipper/BasicInformation (billing and ordering entity mappings), Shipper/SaveKYCDetails, Shipper/CreditInfo/SaveCreditInfo where credit terms exist, and stores the link with the ADEED organisation ID as externalShipperMasterId.
  • FR-2.4 Lanes and wallet: the organisation registers defined lanes or the full wallet and lists current carriers for invitation.
  • Acceptance: an anchor client is usable end to end without any TruKKer credential; provisioning is idempotent (a retry does not create a second client); duplicate organisations are caught by TRN.

FR-3 Carrier, driver and asset onboarding (Registry)

  • FR-3.1 Invitation by tokenised link, Arabic, Urdu and English; mobile-first wizard.
  • FR-3.2 Company KYB: trade licence, operating card, TRN, insurance; fleet by plate and chassis; drivers with Emirates ID, passport, visa, licence; bank details and MoXey opt-in.
  • FR-3.3 Ops verification queue with document, expiry and sanctions checks; approve or reject with reasons; 72-hour SLA countdown per carrier.
  • FR-3.4 On approval the gateway writes Supplier/BasicInfo/BasicInformation, Supplier/kycInfo/SaveKYCDetails, Driver/BasicInfo/BasicInformation, Driver/kycInfo/SaveKYCDetails, Truck/BasicInfo/BasicInformation, TruckDetails/SaveTruckDetails.
  • FR-3.5 Registry credential issued with a quality score v0 (OTIF, POD timeliness, incidents, document hygiene) and expiry alerts at 30 and 7 days with auto-suspend.
  • Acceptance: onboarding completes within 72 hours for the anchor clients' carriers; every verified carrier, driver and truck exists in TruKKer with matching identifiers; suspension propagates.

FR-4 Demand capture

  • FR-4.1 Wizard: lane with map pins, cargo, asset type, schedule, customs mode, pricing mode (contract or spot), documents; templates from previous requests; the field groups follow SaveInquiry.
  • FR-4.2 Completeness checks per move type with missing-information prompts before submission.
  • FR-4.3 Submission persists the request, maps the payload and calls InquiryMaster/SaveInquiry with inquirySource "7X"; the inquiry number is stored on the request.
  • FR-4.4 Track, amend and cancel before confirmation: Reports/DemandTrackerReport, InquiryMaster/GetInquiryById, InquiryMaster/UpdateInquiryDetails, InquiryMaster/CancelInquiry with the disposition reasons.
  • FR-4.5 Confirmed-type demand (inquiryType Confirmed) skips price discovery and goes straight to booking; contract lanes price from the rate card.
  • Acceptance: 90 percent of first submissions pass completeness; every request has a TruKKer inquiry number within one poll cycle; an amend after confirmation is refused with a clear message.

FR-5 Pricing, bidding and confirmation

  • FR-5.1 Spot requests show the bid ledger from LoadboardDashBoard/GetDashboardDetails and GetBidHistory, polled every 1 to 2 minutes while bidding is live; bidders anonymised until award under a policy toggle.
  • FR-5.2 The winner, price, quote reference and currency come from GetInquiryById once TruKKer auto-declares the lowest vendor rate; the inquiry status follows Annex A (Pending, Partially Fulfilled, Fulfilled, Partially Booked, Booked, Cancelled).
  • FR-5.3 Confirmation, pilot: the shipper presses Confirm or Decline in ADEED; ADEED records the decision in the audit trail and raises an ops task; ops confirms in TruKKer; the mirror flips the request to Booked when GetDemandOrdersByInquiryId returns orders. Self-serve write replaces the ops step when TruKKer delivers it (GAP-02).
  • FR-5.4 Savings against the pre-pilot baseline shown to procurement per lane.
  • Acceptance: every bid and the confirming identity appear in the audit trail; time from confirmation to Booked in ADEED is under 15 minutes in the pilot; no request is booked without a recorded confirmation.

FR-6 Booking, assignment and waybill

  • FR-6.1 Orders mirrored as shipments with ADEED references from GetDemandOrdersByInquiryId and OrderMaster/GetOrdersList; details from GetOrderDetailsById; truck and driver from GetTripInfoById.
  • FR-6.2 Waybill v0 issued per movement from the order data: EBN reference, QR, shipper, consignee, cargo, route, carrier, driver, truck; attached to the TruKKer order through the hook agreed under GAP-19; amendment log on reassignment; one waybill per truck on multi-truck orders.
  • FR-6.3 Cancel a confirmed shipment with a disposition reason: OrderMaster/CancelOrder.
  • Acceptance: 100 percent of pilot movements carry a waybill; the QR resolves to a verification page with the current state; a cancelled order closes the waybill.

FR-7 Tracking and exceptions

  • FR-7.1 Timeline from the Annex A statusName sequence (domestic 8 states, cross-border 12), polled from shipment details every 5 minutes; timestamps in ISO 8601 UTC displayed in Gulf time.
  • FR-7.2 Notifications per milestone by email to the shipper's chosen recipients; consignee link for the delivery window.
  • FR-7.3 Exceptions: delay, breakdown, rejection, deviation open an incident in the ops queue with a 1-hour clock; pilot incidents are logged by ops from TruKKer's operations channel until a feed exists (GAP-08).
  • FR-7.4 Positions and ETA arrive with GAP-06; the strip and map are designed for them now and hidden until data exists.
  • Acceptance: every active shipment shows a status no older than one poll cycle; a missed poll raises an integration alert, not a silent stale state.

FR-8 Delivery, POD and the vault

  • FR-8.1 Delivery status from the timeline; POD retrieved with OrderMaster/GetTripDocs and copied on receipt into UAE-resident storage; upload from ADEED with SaveTripDocs once upload custody is settled (GAP-10).
  • FR-8.2 Ops verification of legibility and completeness; disputes open before invoicing; a POD missing past 6 hours triggers a carrier chase with quality-score impact.
  • FR-8.3 Vault per shipment: POD, waybill, Bayan and customs documents (tripDetailsService/v1/sourceDestination), line items (lineitemService/v1/findRevenueLineItems).
  • Acceptance: POD visible in ADEED within 6 hours of delivery for 95 percent of movements; every stored binary has a receipt timestamp and source.

FR-9 Billing and settlement

  • FR-9.1 Uninvoiced queue (invoiceService/v1/unBilledAgeingReport), line-item review, invoice generation across selected orders (saveInvoiceGenerationData, activation subject to the commercial terms).
  • FR-9.2 Invoice per order with PDF: getInvoicesDetailsByOrderNumber; acknowledgement document; unpaid invoices (Collection/GetInvoiceList); statement of account (Communication/PrevStatementOfAccAsync).
  • FR-9.3 Acceptance and disputes (Sprint 2, GAP-12), payments read-back (GAP-13), payment allocation (Collection/AllocatePayment) once writes activate.
  • FR-9.4 Carrier settlement visibility with MoXey status (Sprint 3, GAP-13).
  • Acceptance: a shipper finance user can find, open and download every invoice for a period in under a minute; turning writes on changes nothing on screen, only gateway behaviour.

FR-10 Ops console

  • FR-10.1 Network board: active requests and shipments, SLA countdowns (pricing, 6-hour POD, 1-hour incident), exception queue.
  • FR-10.2 Registry queue with the 72-hour SLA and expiry radar.
  • FR-10.3 Integration health: sync-job status, error rates, nightly reconciliation differences, the immutable integration log.
  • FR-10.4 Ops-mediated actions with an audit trail: confirmation recorded, incident logged, waybill attached, POD verified.
  • Acceptance: any SLA at risk is visible on the board at least 30 minutes before breach; every ops action is attributable.

FR-11 Programme dashboard

  • FR-11.1 One page: the Section 2.3 measures with definitions, current value, trend and data freshness; savings against baseline; formalisation metrics (carriers and drivers verified).
  • FR-11.2 Print and PDF export for the monthly brief; fortnightly working-group view.
  • Acceptance: the page is readable in under a minute and every figure links to its source view.

FR-12 Regulator views (Phase 1)

Registry search, waybill verification by QR, compliance statistics, PII minimised per role. Designed in Sprint 4 as a preview, built in Phase 1.

FR-13 Notifications

  • Email through the existing notification engine with freight templates in English and Arabic; recipients per organisation and event; digest option.
  • In-app notifications derived from the shipment and request event tables, with unread counts.
  • WhatsApp is a later channel through an approved business line; not in the pilot.

FR-14 Search and Copilot

  • Global search across requests, shipments, documents and invoices by reference, lane, carrier or date.
  • Copilot (Sprint 4): conversational capture that produces a governed draft request using the same validation as the wizard; quote, book and track as callable tools.

FR-15 Partner API freight scopes (Sprint 4)

  • freight:requests, freight:shipments, freight:documents, freight:invoices scopes on the existing /api/v1 key model, PII-free DTOs, idempotency keys on request creation, for client ERPs (SAP, Oracle).

8. Integration Gateway requirements

The gateway is the only component that talks to TruKKer. It is a set of typed outbound clients, polling workers and mappers inside the ADEED platform.

8.1 Responsibilities

  • Token custody per environment; never in client code; OAuth as confirmed by TruKKer.
  • Payload mapping between ADEED entities and the TruKKer contracts, including the 90-field SaveInquiry payload and the v2 onboarding payloads.
  • Polling: open requests every 1 to 2 minutes while bidding is live; in-transit shipments every 5 minutes; finance nightly; a daily reconciliation of order and invoice counts.
  • Idempotency keys on every write; retries with backoff; a repeat call must return the original result (agreed by TruKKer).
  • An immutable integration log of every call in both directions: endpoint, identifiers, status, duration, correlation ID.
  • Copy on receipt: every document binary retrieved from TruKKer is stored in UAE-resident storage the moment it is fetched.

8.2 Identifier convention

The ADEED organisation ID is the externalShipperMasterId join key on every request in both directions (confirmed by TruKKer). The gateway maps it onto the four field spellings TruKKer still uses (externalShipperMasterId, shipperMasterId, extShipperId, shipperId) until the conventions annex unifies them. Orders keep the TruKKer orderNumber and an ADEED reference; inquiries keep inquiryNumber.

8.3 Status mapping

10. Technical foundations

The portal is built inside the existing ADEED platform and follows its conventions. The survey below states what exists and what this product adds.

10.1 Existing conventions to follow

  • Next.js 15.5 App Router, React 18.3, TypeScript strict, Tailwind 3.4 with cn(); client pages call route handlers under src/app/api, Prisma 6 on Azure Postgres; Zustand for client state; framer-motion for motion; lucide-react icons; recharts for charts; Leaflet and Mapbox already present for maps.
  • Locale segment /[locale]/ with en and ar, custom dictionaries in src/i18n/en.ts and ar.ts, useTranslation() and the locale layout that sets dir and the Arabic font. Freight adds freight-en.ts and freight-ar.ts dictionaries; no inline label maps.
  • Sessions: the shared adeed_session cookie; a new src/lib/freight-session.ts mirrors finance-session.ts (participant gate, 8-hour idle timeout, requireFreightRole, ensureFreightParticipant); a FreightPortalShell mirrors FinancePortalShell.
  • Partner API: withApiKey, the response envelope, the DTO boundary in api-dto.ts, ApiRequestLog.
  • Timeline pattern: ShipmentEvent copies FinanceApplicationEvent; audit copies FinanceAuditLog.
  • Deployment: GitHub Actions to the Azure Web App with prisma migrate deploy at boot; a proper migration is required for the freight models (recent models were applied by db push; freight must not repeat that).

11. Non-functional requirements

  • Security. OAuth tokens in server-side custody; least-privilege roles; same-origin checks on mutations; PII minimised per role (regulator views never see contact details); document access only through signed, audited routes.
  • Residency. All user, registry, waybill and transactional data, and every document copy, in the 7X-procured UAE tenancy per LOI clause 2.
  • Performance. Portal pages interactive under 2 seconds on a normal connection; lists paginated at 25; the shipment strip renders without layout shift; poll workloads sized to TruKKer's published rate limits.
  • Reliability. Gateway retries with backoff; idempotent writes; nightly reconciliation with alerts; no silent stale states (every status carries its age).
  • Accessibility. WCAG 2.2 AA in both languages; keyboard paths through every flow; reduced motion honoured.
  • Auditability. Every confirmation, verification, incident and document receipt attributable to an actor with a timestamp; integration log immutable.
  • Browser support. Current Chrome, Safari, Edge and Firefox on desktop; iOS Safari and Android Chrome for the carrier wizard.

B. Pilot measure definitions

On-time delivery: delivered timestamp at or before the planned delivery window end. Track and trace: a movement with a status update no older than one poll cycle for its entire in-transit period. Digital POD: POD document present in the vault within 6 hours of the delivered timestamp. Incident reporting: incident opened within 60 minutes of the triggering event. Carrier onboarding: verified within 72 hours of invitation acceptance. Waybill coverage: movements with a waybill in state issued or later at departure.
